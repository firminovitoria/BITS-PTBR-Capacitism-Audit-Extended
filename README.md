# Automated Ableism Revisited: BITS-PTBR v2

Code, data, and results for the article "Automated Ableism Revisited: Specificity, Origins, and External Validity of Disability Bias in Portuguese Text Classifiers", submitted to the *Journal on Interactive Systems* (JIS). The article is an extended and revised version of "Capacitismo Automatizado: Uma Auditoria de Modelos de Linguagem em Português nas Tarefas de Sentimento e Toxicidade" (WICS 2026).

The original study adapted the BITS template set of Venkit et al. (2023) to Brazilian Portuguese (BITS-PTBR) and used Perturbation Sensitivity Analysis (PSA; Prabhakaran et al., 2019) to measure disability bias in one sentiment classifier and two toxicity classifiers. The extended version asks whether the effect is specific to disability, where it may come from, and whether it appears beyond the templates.

File and notebook names are in Portuguese, as in the original project. The corpus terms are also kept in Portuguese, as they appear in the sentences.

## Research questions

- **RQ1.** Is the effect observed in model predictions specific to disability references, or does it also occur when other personal characteristics are mentioned?
- **RQ2.** To what extent can literal, figurative, and pejorative uses of disability-related terms help explain the differences observed in model predictions?
- **RQ3.** How do the observed effects vary across terms, groups, sentence structures (templates), and emotional categories?
- **RQ4.** Do the false-positive patterns identified in the template sentences also occur in non-toxic sentences from other corpora that mention people with disabilities?
- **RQ5.** Could the differences observed among toxicity classifiers be associated with how disability-related terms appear in their training data? (descriptive, not causal)

Each pair contains the sentence with the term (`Sentence`) and the control without it (`Sentence_control`). The difference is score(sentence with the term) − score(control).

**v2 groups:** Clinical (6 terms), Social Discourse (10), Non-Disabled (4), NRMA (5), Age (3), Nationality (3), Religion (3), and Length Control (7). Age terms are not combined with templates T1 and T9, which refer to a child, and "idoso" (elderly) is also not combined with T12, which uses "rapaz" (young man). The Age group therefore has 117 or 118 pairs per term, and comparisons involving it use only the shared controls.

## Decision thresholds

Chosen on half A of HateBR (seed 42), evaluated on half B, and validated on the 2,010 ToLD-Br test tweets not seen by the models (`results/thresholds.json`). A decision is toxic when the score is greater than or equal to the threshold.

| | 0.5 (WICS) | Youden | 5% false positives |
|---|---|---|---|
| BERTimbau toxic | 0.500 | 0.797 | 0.841 |
| ToxiGuardrail-PT | 0.500 | 0.432 | 0.877 |

The 5% false-positive threshold is used in the main analyses; the others are used as a sensitivity analysis. Thresholds affect only the binary metrics (FlipRate and LabelDist); scores and differences do not depend on them.

## Metrics

- **ScoreSense:** mean of the differences in a group.
- **ScoreDev:** for each context, the population standard deviation of the scores of the sentences with each term of the group, averaged over contexts.
- **FlipRate:** proportion of pairs whose decision changes between the control and the sentence with the term, reported by direction (to toxic and to non-toxic). For sentiment, any change among the three classes is counted.
- **LabelDist:** Jaccard distance between the sets of pairs in the class of interest with and without the term, computed over all pairs of a group. The class of interest is the toxic class for the toxicity models and the negative class for the sentiment model. It saturates at 0 or 1 when the controls have no positive cases, as in ToxiGuardrail-PT.
- **False-positive rate:** among sentences labeled as non-toxic, the fraction classified as toxic.
- **Intervals:** 95% percentile bootstrap with 10,000 resamples and seed 42, by context (control sentence) and by context and term (two-level). Group contrasts are paired by context, with Holm correction.

## Notes

- The `entra_no_v2` column of `lista_termos_congelada.csv` records the status on the day the list was frozen (October 12, 2026). The four terms marked as pending ("autista", "deficiente", "deficiente mental", and "em cadeira de rodas") were later included in v2.
- Notebook 07 also contains a pair-level bootstrap, which treats pairs as independent. It is kept for the record; the analyses in the article use the two-level bootstrap of notebook 09.
- In the literal × figurative subset, the literal control removes the term, whereas the figurative control replaces it with a paraphrase. For this reason, the two uses are compared by the absolute score of the sentences with the term (notebook 09).
- The classification of "cego" and "surdo" (notebook 11) was carried out independently by two of the authors and then consolidated (`results/e_classificacao_cego_surdo.csv`).
